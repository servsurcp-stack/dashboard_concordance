# **📌 Technical Debt Identified in the Architecture**

Below is a consolidated list of **structural, technological, operational, and governance-related technical debt** that emerges from the architecture as described.

---

## **1. Legacy / Self‑Managed Infrastructure Components**
Several critical services are **self‑hosted on EC2**, which creates long‑term operational debt.

### **1.1 Active Directory, DNS, DHCP, SCCM, PKI, KMS all self‑managed on EC2**
> “La plateforme intègre un Active Directory Microsoft autogéré sur EC2…”  
> “DNS Microsoft… DHCP… SCCM sur EC2 Windows… PKI Windows…”  

**Debt created:**
- High maintenance burden (patching, backups, HA, DR).
- Not cloud‑native → increases operational risk.
- Harder to scale and automate.
- Requires Windows licensing and lifecycle management.

### **1.2 On‑premise‑style services recreated in AWS**
- Printing servers on EC2  
- SCCM for workstation management  
- Windows KMS activation service  

These are **not aligned with modern cloud‑native patterns**.

---

## **2. Heavy Dependence on Talend (ESB + ETL)**
The architecture centralizes almost everything around Talend.

> “Talend ESB constitue le point central de médiation…”  
> “Talend ETL prend en charge les traitements…”  

**Debt created:**
- ESB‑centric architectures are known to become bottlenecks.
- Vendor lock‑in on Talend for both integration and data pipelines.
- ESB patterns are less scalable than event‑driven microservices.
- Risk of monolithic integration layer.
- Harder to evolve toward serverless or streaming‑native patterns.

---

## **3. Multi‑Layer Replication Chain for WMS/TMS**
The parcel operational data flows through **three layers of replication**:

MAWM (SaaS) → CEVA → AWS replica

> “La base CEVA… réplique de MAWM… Une réplication de second niveau… vers AWS.”

**Debt created:**
- Complex replication chain → fragile, latency‑prone.
- Hard to troubleshoot data inconsistencies.
- Multi‑vendor dependency (Manhattan + CEVA + AWS).
- Increased RPO/RTO risk.

---

## **4. Over‑Centralization of API Gateway as Single Entry Point**
> “Tout flux entrant… doit obligatoirement passer par Amazon API Gateway.”

**Debt created:**
- Single choke point for all external traffic.
- Risk of global outage if misconfigured.
- Hard to scale for heterogeneous workloads (webhooks, bulk ingestion, etc.).
- No mention of multi‑region API Gateway or failover strategy.

---

## **5. Over‑Reliance on Angular Frontends + Shared Library**
> “Les frontaux… sont développés en Angular… une librairie en surcouche…”

**Debt created:**
- Shared UI library → coupling between domains.
- Angular monorepo‑like patterns can slow independent deployments.
- Mobile apps generated from Angular wrappers → technical compromise.

---

## **6. Lack of Cloud‑Native Identity & Access Management**
The architecture relies heavily on **Windows AD** instead of AWS IAM, AWS SSO, or Cognito.

**Debt created:**
- AD on EC2 is not scalable or resilient.
- No mention of federation with AWS IAM Identity Center.
- Increased operational overhead and security risk.

---

## **7. SFTP as a Required Integration Channel**
> “Le socle d’intégration doit proposer une capacité d’échange SFTP…”

**Debt created:**
- SFTP is legacy, stateful, and operationally heavy.
- Requires key rotation, storage, monitoring, retries.
- Encourages partners to avoid modern API‑based integration.

---

## **8. BI Architecture Split Between AWS and Microsoft**
> “Power BI… capacité Microsoft Fabric F64…”  
> “Option alternative QuickSight…”

**Debt created:**
- Hybrid BI stack increases complexity.
- Power BI requires data egress from AWS → cost + latency.
- Governance split between AWS and Microsoft ecosystems.

---

## **9. Rekognition Used for Face Detection but Not for Parcel Detection**
> “La détection de visage… la plus fiable…  
> La reconnaissance… de la présence effective d’un colis doit être considérée avec prudence…”

**Debt created:**
- Incomplete automation of photo validation.
- Manual fallback processes likely required.
- Future rework needed when parcel detection becomes reliable.

---

## **10. Multi‑AZ Strategy Without Multi‑Region Considerations**
> “L’architecture s’appuie sur deux Availability Zones…”

**Debt created:**
- No disaster recovery outside the region.
- AZ‑level resilience ≠ regional resilience.
- Critical for logistics operations requiring 24/7 uptime.

---

## **11. ESB + ETL + MSK = Three Integration Paradigms**
The architecture mixes:
- ESB (synchronous mediation)
- ETL (batch)
- Kafka/MSK (asynchronous)

**Debt created:**
- Complex integration landscape.
- Harder onboarding for developers.
- Risk of duplicated logic across layers.
- Increased operational cost.

---

## **12. No Mention of Infrastructure as Code (IaC)**
The document does not reference:
- Terraform  
- CloudFormation  
- CDK  

**Debt created:**
- Risk of manual deployments.
- Harder to reproduce environments.
- Increased configuration drift.

---

## **13. No Mention of Observability Stack Beyond Zabbix**
> “Supervision… s’appuyant sur Zabbix…”

**Debt created:**
- Zabbix is not cloud‑native.
- No mention of:
  - distributed tracing (X‑Ray, OpenTelemetry)
  - structured logging
  - centralized dashboards (Grafana)
  - log analytics (CloudWatch Logs Insights)

---

## **14. Strong Coupling to Salesforce, SAP, Manhattan, CEVA**
The architecture depends on multiple SaaS vendors with proprietary integration models.

**Debt created:**
- High integration maintenance cost.
- Risk of API changes breaking flows.
- Hard to evolve toward event‑driven architecture.
